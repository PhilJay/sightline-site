To screen record with your webcam on a Mac, choose a camera in Sightline and record as usual. You appear in a webcam bubble, and since the camera is recorded next to the screen, it stays editable in the video editor.
  • Pick a cameraIn Settings › Recording, choose one under Camera and allow Camera access.
  • RecordPress ⇧⌘7 and drag an area, or choose Record Window… or Record Full Screen, then click Record.
  • Stop and editPress ⇧⌘7 again and open the recording from its thumbnail.
    •

    In the editor the camera gets its own Camera row in the timeline, next to the clips and the sound.

  • Move and resize the camera in the video, part by part
  • Split the Camera row with ⌘B and give each part its own place
  • Hide a part with ⌫ or Hide Camera in the right-click menu
  • Shape: Circle or Rounded Rectangle, with Corners and a Shadow
  • Glide smoothly between places, like a zoom
  • Stay in sync: crop and zoom never move it, cuts keep it with your voice
    •

    Save or Export draws the camera in, as MP4, MOV or GIF. Save Camera Video… writes the camera on its own.
  • Up to 4K at 60 fps, as far as your camera offers it, 720p at 30 fps by default
  • Your voice and system audio in the same recording
  • Hide camera in the recording controls stops filming until you show it
  • Zooms, keystrokes and blurs that follow what they cover, in the same editor
    •

    Every option is in the camera section of the recording docs.

    macOS has no switch for a webcam overlay, but two built-in tools come close (Apple Support, as of 27 November 2026).

  • QuickTime Player: File › New Movie Recording shows your camera, View › Float on Top keeps it in front
  • Presenter Overlay: puts you over a screen you share in a video call, macOS 14 or later, Apple silicon
    •

    Both are free. With QuickTime the camera is baked into the picture, so it can’t move later.
