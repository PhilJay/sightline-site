Screenshots

Screenshots that explain themselves.

Capture and annotate

Capture. Mark it up.

Area, window and full screen with the shortcuts you already know, a magnifier for pixel exact edges and a floating thumbnail. While you drag, ⌘C, ⌘S, ⌘P and ⌘B copy, save, pin or cut out the background right away.

The editor draws arrows, shapes, text, highlights, spotlight, magnify, blur and step numbers, each on a single key. It also opens the PNG, JPEG or MP4 files you already have.

Remove Background

Lift the subject.

Remove Background cuts a capture's subject out onto a transparent background. Press ⌘B while you drag, or use the tool in the editor: click what you want to keep, click again to add more, Option click to take away.

Apple's on-device Vision model. Needs macOS 27.

Scrolling capture

Capture the whole page.

Long pages, chat logs and Terminal output become one tall image. Sightline scrolls for you, or you scroll yourself, up or down, and it follows along and stitches the frames together.

Beautify

Ready to share in one click.

Beautify puts a screenshot on a background with padding, rounded corners and a soft shadow. Pick a look, or choose a gradient, a color or your own picture, and a size from Auto to 16:9 or 1:1.

Save a look as a preset, or use it for every new screenshot.

Recordings

Record a screencast. Cut it into shape.

Record the screen with your voice, then edit it on a real timeline: clips, sound, zooms and a row for every mark. Saved recordings stay editable when you open them again.

Your voice and a countdown

Record your microphone with the screen. A countdown gets you ready, and the recording bar pauses or mutes you.

Clips and speed

Split with ⌘B, trim, drag clips into a new order and play one at 0.5x up to 15x.

Sound you can shape

Split the sound, mute a part or raise it up to 400 %.

Zoom in, smoothly

Draw a frame and the video eases in on it. Every zoom gets a bar to move and trim.

Blur that follows

Cover an email address once, and the blur stays on it while the page scrolls. Every mark has its own time.

Replay

Keep the last 15 seconds of your screen and save what just happened with ⌘⇧8.

Needs macOS 27.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Can Claude Code or Codex take screenshots on my Mac?

Yes. Sightline has a built in MCP server, so Claude Code, Claude Desktop, Codex CLI and other MCP clients can capture the screen, a window or an area, read the text on it and mark it up. Turn on agent access and click Connect in Settings > AI Agents.

Can my AI agent check its own UI work?

Yes. Your agent builds something, captures the window and looks at the result, so it notices when a button sits off.

Which AI agents work with Sightline?

Claude Code, Claude Desktop and Codex CLI connect with one click. Any other MCP client works by running /Applications/Sightline.app/Contents/MacOS/sightline-cli mcp as a stdio server.

What can an AI agent see and do on my Mac?

Only what you allow. Agent access is off until you turn it on, each new agent asks first, and you can remove an agent at any time in Settings. Agents can't change settings and only read and write inside your screenshot folder.

Does Sightline replace the macOS screenshot tool?

It works the same way, with ⌘⇧3, ⌘⇧4 and ⌘⇧5 and the thumbnail in the corner, and adds an editor, scrolling capture, text recognition, Remove Background, pins, a history, and screen recording with your voice, a video editor and blur that follows.

Do my screenshots leave my Mac?

No. Captures stay on your Mac, and text recognition, naming and the password check run on device with Apple Intelligence and Apple's Vision. There is no account, no analytics and no server of its own.

Does Sightline use Apple Intelligence?

Yes, on Macs that support it. Apple Intelligence names your screenshots after what is in them and finds passwords, keys and tokens before you share, all on device with macOS 26 or later. Remove Background and the subject blur use Apple's on-device Vision model on macOS 27.

Can Sightline record my screen?

Yes. Record an area, a window or the whole display to MP4 with system audio and, if you like, your microphone, after an optional countdown. The recording opens in an editor where you split, reorder and speed up clips, mute or raise parts of the sound, and draw arrows, text and blurs that each show for their own stretch of time. A blur stays on an email address or a face while the page scrolls. On macOS 27, Replay keeps the last 15 seconds so you can save what just happened.

Can I record screencasts with my voice?

Yes. Turn on Record microphone in Settings › Recording and pick a microphone. Your voice is mixed with the system sound into the recording, and the recording bar mutes the microphone or pauses the recording while you talk. Afterwards you cut the screencast into shape in the editor and share it.

What can I do in the editor?

Draw arrows, shapes, text, highlights, a spotlight, a magnifier, blur and step numbers, each on a single key. Crop, remove the background, paste and arrange several captures, and pin a capture over your other windows. Recordings get a timeline with clips, a sound row and a row for every mark. Every mark stays editable, also after you save and open the file again, including the ones your AI agent drew.

Which macOS versions does it need?

macOS 14 or later. Screen recording needs macOS 15, the Apple Intelligence features macOS 26, and Remove Background and Replay macOS 27.
